# Alternatives and comparison pages

People ask engines "best <competitor> alternative" and "<A> vs <B>". A page that answers
honestly gets cited; a page that reads like an ad gets cited inside answers that recommend
someone else. Citations are not recommendations: shortlist membership comes from third-party
consensus (reviews, forums, press), which no page on your own site can manufacture.

## When to build one

- The competitor is one buyers actually compare against (search suggestions, sales calls, the
  product's own positioning), and the product genuinely overlaps on the job.
- Every own-product claim is on the claims ledger with a file:line.
- Prefer one page per real competitor over a page per phrasing. "X alternative", "X
  alternatives" and "X vs us" are one intent; build one page and let headings cover the rest.

## URL and title

- One stable pattern, for example `/compare/<competitor>-alternative`, linked from a comparison
  hub. Title near 60 characters with the competitor's name first.

## Structure

1. A two to three sentence answer up top: who should pick which, and why.
2. A comparison table: price, platforms, the jobs both do, the jobs only one does. Use
   yes, no, and partial with the catch written out.
3. "Where <competitor> is better", said plainly. This is what makes the page citable.
4. Who each product is best for.
5. Switching notes when relevant (what carries over, what does not).
6. FAQ answering the real follow-up questions, visible on the page.
7. "Checked on <date>" with links to the competitor's own pricing and docs pages.

## Honesty rules, hard

- Date every competitor fact and link its source. Re-check before each republish.
- Never claim absence from one visit. "Not listed on their pricing page as of <date>" or omit.
- Separate what their site says from interpretation.
- Never name competitors' customers, use their logos, or quote their reviews out of context.
- Competitors read these pages. Every line must survive them checking it.

## Measured context

- In mid 2026 ChatGPT shifted citations away from listicles and generic comparison pages toward
  official and primary-source pages (Profound, reported August 2026). An honest page on the
  product's own domain is the primary source for its half of the comparison; a "top 10" listicle
  on that domain is not.
